Netflix is betting big on India with its new six‑part series Operation Safed Sagar, a dramatized retelling of the 1999 Kargil War. The show promises unprecedented access to Indian Air Force bases and high‑octane aerial combat filmed at altitudes near 16,000 feet. With more than 1,200 local visual‑effects artists contributing 50 minutes of combat sequences, the production is billed as Netflix’s largest operational undertaking in the country. Analysts see the series as a strategic move to cement Netflix’s premium position in the world’s most populous market.
<Callout type="takeaway">Operation Safed Sagar is Netflix’s most ambitious Indian original, designed to win viewers and market share.</Callout>
## What happened
Netflix announced the six‑part series Operation Safed Sagar, which dramatizes the Indian Air Force’s role in the 1999 Kargil War. The series was produced with “unprecedented” access to IAF bases, allowing filmmakers to shoot at altitudes of nearly 16,000 feet. More than 1,200 local visual‑effects artists created roughly 50 minutes of aerial combat sequences, and the production injected over $24 million into the Indian economy.
## Why it matters
The ambitious scale signals Netflix’s intent to compete with domestic streaming giants by offering high‑budget, locally resonant content. By spotlighting a defining moment in Indo‑Pak relations, the series may attract viewers seeking both entertainment and a sense of national pride, while also testing the platform’s ability to navigate politically sensitive narratives.
