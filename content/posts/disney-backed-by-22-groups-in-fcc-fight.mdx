A coalition of 22 civil liberties and free speech organizations has lined up behind Disney in its escalating dispute with the Federal Communications Commission. The coalition argues that the company's legal fight with the FCC carries implications far beyond a single media company. The dispute began when the FCC ordered Disney to seek early license renewals for its eight owned-and-operated ABC television stations. Disney has challenged the FCC's actions, calling the early renewal order unlawful and unconstitutional. The coalition is urging Disney to continue fighting for First Amendment rights.

   ## What happened
   The FCC ordered Disney to seek early license renewals for its eight owned-and-operated ABC television stations, years ahead of schedule. Disney has challenged the FCC's actions in filings with the Commission, calling the early renewal order unlawful and unconstitutional. The company has also argued that "The View" qualifies for the FCC's longstanding news exemption under federal equal-time requirements.
   The coalition of 22 civil liberties and free speech organizations has written an open letter to Disney CEO Josh D'Amaro, urging the company to continue fighting for First Amendment rights. The coalition includes the American Civil Liberties Union, the National Coalition Against Censorship, PEN America, the Electronic Frontier Foundation, Public Knowledge, and TechFreedom.
   The dispute between Disney and the FCC has significant implications for the media industry and the public's First Amendment rights.
