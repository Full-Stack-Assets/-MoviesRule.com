Collider just published a fresh ranking of the eight Pokémon games every fan should own. The list spans the series from the Game Boy origins to the latest Switch experiments, highlighting why each title still feels essential. By grouping classic, handheld, and modern entries, the article gives both newcomers and long‑time trainers a clear roadmap. Whether you prefer turn‑based strategy or open‑world exploration, the ranking shows how the franchise has evolved while keeping its core charm. Understanding this hierarchy helps players choose the right entry point for their next adventure.

## What happened
Collider’s article enumerates eight titles that represent key milestones in the franchise. The lineup starts with the original Pokémon Red and Blue, the games that launched the series on the Game Boy, and moves through the Game Boy Color era with Gold and Silver. It then highlights the GameCube experiments Pokémon Colosseum and its sequel XD: Gale of Darkness, before covering the 3DS era’s Sun and Moon, the Switch‑era open‑world experiment Legends: Z‑A, and the modern remake New Pokémon Snap. Each entry is praised for its unique contribution—whether it be pioneering mechanics, narrative depth, or innovative use of hardware.
The ranking balances nostalgia with gameplay innovation. Collider notes that while older titles may suffer from dated graphics and limited online features, they lay the foundation for later design choices. Conversely, newer games like Legends: Z‑A and New Pokémon Snap are celebrated for reimagining combat and photography within the Pokémon world, proving the series can still surprise long‑time fans.
## Why it matters
These eight games act as a curated syllabus for anyone looking to experience the full breadth of Pokémon’s design evolution. For streaming watchers, the titles provide varied content—from classic speedruns of Red/Blue to cinematic open‑world hunts in Legends: Z‑A—making them ideal for different audience tastes. Moreover, the list underscores how the franchise has remained culturally relevant, influencing everything from collectible card games to global pop culture.

## How to think about it
When choosing a game, start by asking what experience you value most. If you crave nostalgia and simple turn‑based battles, begin with Red/Blue or Gold/Silver. For players who enjoy open worlds and real‑time action, Legends: Z‑A is the logical next step. Those interested in side activities like photography should try New Pokémon Snap. Consider platform availability, multiplayer needs, and how much time you can invest, then let the ranking serve as a flexible checklist rather than a strict order.
<Callout type="warning">Skipping the original Red/Blue may leave you missing core series concepts that later games reference.</Callout>
