On Sunday afternoon at American Family Field, the Milwaukee Brewers and Minnesota Twins battled to a 3‑3 tie. Both clubs dealt with roster shuffles—Milwaukee inserted Andrew Vaughn at third and David Hamilton at shortstop, while Minnesota relied on rookie left‑hander Connor Prielipp. The game featured a clutch two‑out homer by Kody Clemens that erased a two‑run deficit, but defensive miscues in the seventh inning kept the tie alive. The result leaves the series undecided and highlights how injuries and lineup tweaks can swing momentum in a tight division race.

## What happened
Milwaukee starter Jacob Misiorowski (11-5, 1.63) returned after a week off and allowed four hits, three runs and a walk while striking out nine over 94 pitches. He walked Josh Bell in the sixth, and Kody Clemens followed with a 99.1‑mph homer that tied the game at 3‑3. In the seventh, a series of defensive errors—Turang’s throwing error and Hamilton losing his footing—prevented the Brewers from capitalizing on a loaded bases situation.

Minnesota’s Connor Prielipp (3-5, 4.75) gave up only one hit through four innings, a single by Brooks Lee that ricocheted off Misiorowski’s left calf. Lee later homered in the fifth, providing the Twins’ only other run. A high fly ball by Lee was caught at the wall by Luis Lara for the final out, preserving the tie.

## Why it matters
The tie keeps the Brewers in a position to clinch the series but underscores how defensive consistency is crucial when pitching is solid. Injuries to Joey Ortiz and Cooper Pratt forced lineup adjustments that may have disrupted chemistry, evident in the seventh‑inning errors. For the Twins, the early dominance of their starter and Lee’s timely hitting keep them competitive despite a struggling bullpen.
