CBS Sports announced Tuesday that veteran NFL analyst Tony Romo has been placed on leave following a July 23 arrest in Milwaukee for suspected operating a vehicle while intoxicated. The incident, captured on body‑camera footage, also noted an open bottle of alcohol, unsafe passing, and refusal to submit to a sobriety test. Romo, a former Dallas Cowboys quarterback and a key voice in the network’s NFL coverage, will remain off the air pending a court appearance on September 21. The timing is critical, as the NFL regular season kicks off on September 9, just weeks before his hearing. Viewers and advertisers are watching closely to see how CBS will adjust its broadcast lineup.

## What happened
On July 23, Milwaukee police stopped Romo’s SUV and cited him for operating a vehicle while intoxicated, an open alcohol container, unsafe passing on the right, and refusing a breath test. He was released later that night and is scheduled to appear in court on September 21.

CBS released a statement confirming Romo is on leave from his analyst duties until further notice. The network has not announced a replacement for the upcoming preseason game on Aug. 28, though it has already shifted J.J. Watt to the lead broadcast team.

## Why it matters
Romo’s absence forces CBS to reshuffle its NFL broadcast team at a crucial moment, potentially affecting on‑air chemistry and viewer expectations. The incident also highlights the broader risk for networks when high‑profile talent faces legal issues, as advertisers and audiences may question the brand’s standards. Additionally, Romo’s $17 million‑per‑year contract adds financial stakes to any prolonged leave or termination.
