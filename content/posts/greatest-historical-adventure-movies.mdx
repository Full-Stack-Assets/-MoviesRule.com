## What happened
Collider’s article assembled the ten greatest historical adventure movies based on criteria such as monumental production values, period accuracy, and narrative momentum. At the summit sits Christopher Nolan’s "The Odyssey" (2026), an IMAX‑rich adaptation of Homer’s epic that pairs Matt Damon’s Odysseus with mythic set pieces like the Cyclops encounter and the siege of Troy. The list’s runner‑up is the 1975 classic "The Man Who Would Be King," praised for its playful tone and subtle commentary on imperial ambition.

The remaining eight titles cover a broad sweep of eras, from ancient civilizations to early‑20th‑century conflicts, mixing iconic Hollywood epics with newer, globally‑produced blockbusters. Each entry is noted for its ability to blend historical context with adventurous storytelling, making the collection a cross‑generational showcase of the genre.

## How to think about it
Start by identifying which era or theme excites you most—ancient myth, medieval conquest, or early‑modern exploration. Check your preferred streaming service for availability; many of the top titles are now part of major libraries, while a few may require rental or purchase. Pair a newer entry like "The Odyssey" with a classic such as "The Man Who Would Be King" to experience how storytelling techniques have evolved. Finally, consider supplementing your viewing with a brief historical overview to separate cinematic embellishment from factual context.
