Dragon Age: The Veilguard finally gave players full access to every companion's active abilities early in the campaign. A recent guide has compiled the strongest skill combos, equipment choices, and role recommendations for all seven allies. Understanding these builds lets you maximize party synergy, especially the Primer‑Detonator mechanic that amplifies damage. The guide also explains how to respec companions without cost, keeping your party adaptable. Applying these strategies can turn tough encounters into manageable fights.

<Callout type="takeaway">Pair a Primer ability with a Detonator on each companion for the biggest damage spikes.</Callout>

## What happened
Each companion can equip three active skills at any time, and the guide recommends always having one Primer and one Detonator active. Primer abilities apply status effects such as Sundered, Weakened, or Overwhelmed, while Detonator abilities deal extra damage to enemies afflicted by those effects. The third slot can be tuned toward damage, support, or a signature ultimate unlocked after completing the companion’s personal quest.

Equipment for each companion is best sourced from their associated faction merchant once that merchant’s inventory is upgraded through quest completion. While any loot can be used, faction‑specific gear provides the highest stat boosts and synergizes with the companion’s class role. The guide lists the top weapon, armor, and accessory for each ally, noting where to find them during exploration.

All non‑warrior companions (Davrin and Taash excluded) can heal the player character, and every companion can apply a temporary buff to Rook. These healing and buff abilities become crucial during longer battles, especially when combined with the Primer‑Detonator damage cycle.

## Why it matters
The Primer‑Detonator loop creates exponential damage spikes that can shorten fights against high‑HP enemies, directly impacting game pacing and resource consumption. Free respeccing means players can experiment with different roles without penalty, encouraging a more dynamic party composition. However, relying heavily on status‑based damage can leave the team vulnerable to enemies that cleanse or resist those effects, making strategic flexibility essential.

## How to think about it
Start by assigning a Primer and a Detonator to every companion, then choose the third skill based on the role you need most in a given encounter—damage, crowd control, or utility. Prioritize faction‑specific gear that enhances the companion’s primary function, but keep a backup set of generic high‑damage weapons for early‑game flexibility. Use the free respeccing option whenever you notice a skill synergy isn’t clicking; swapping a support skill for an additional damage skill can dramatically change fight outcomes. Finally, monitor enemy resistances and be ready to pivot to pure damage or defensive builds if status effects are ineffective.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid locking all three active slots into damage‑only skills; you’ll lose crucial healing and buff options that become vital in longer battles.</Callout>
