After 19 seasons of the flagship NCIS, the prequel NCIS: Origins is gearing up for a high‑stakes return. Season 3 is slated to debut on Tuesday, October 6, 2026 at 10 p.m. ET, kicking off a ten‑episode fall run. Mark Harmon, the iconic Gibbs, will appear in every episode, anchoring a season‑long mystery tied to his 1990s Camp Pendleton days. The shift to a single showrunner and a tighter episode order promise a more focused narrative.

<Callout type="takeaway">Season 3 delivers a ten‑episode arc that puts Mark Harmon front‑and‑center for the first time.</Callout>

## What happened
NCIS: Origins returns on October 6, 2026, airing Tuesdays at 10/9c, with the new spinoff NCIS: New York taking the 9/8c slot. The season will consist of ten episodes, a reduction from the 18‑episode orders of the first two seasons, according to Deadline.

The core cast returns—Austin Stowell as Gibbs, Mariel Molino, Kyle Schmid, Tyla Abercrumbie, Diany Rodriguez, and Caleb Foote—while Mark Harmon, who has narrated each episode, will now appear on‑screen in every installment. The series also moves to a single showrunner after Gina Lucita Monreal departs, leaving David J. North in charge of the third season.

## Why it matters
Mark Harmon’s expanded presence transforms the series from a peripheral tie‑in to a central narrative driver, likely drawing longtime Gibbs fans back to the prequel. A ten‑episode format forces tighter storytelling, which could raise critical reception and keep viewers engaged week‑by‑week. The showrunner change may also shift the tonal balance, affecting how the series differentiates itself from the main NCIS franchise and its new sibling, NCIS: New York.
