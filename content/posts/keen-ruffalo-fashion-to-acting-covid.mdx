When the COVID-19 pandemic shut down campuses, 25-year-old Keen Ruffalo was studying fashion design, not acting. A bout of depression and a search for purpose led him to enroll in multiple conservatories and study the craft his father, Mark Ruffalo, had made famous. After his dad saw him perform in a play and gave his blessing, Keen landed starring roles in Prime Video’s YA series “Sterling Point” and the upcoming comedy “Nimrods.” His sudden shift from runway sketches to a screen debut highlights how the pandemic reshaped career trajectories for a new generation of talent.

<Callout type="takeaway">The pandemic pushed Keen Ruffalo from fashion school into acting, launching his career on Prime Video.</Callout>

## What happened
Keen grew up on a farm in upstate New York, far from the Hollywood circles that defined his father’s career. When COVID forced campus closures, he found himself depressed and uncertain about his future, prompting a deep dive into acting classes at several conservatories. He described the experience as an instant love affair with the craft.

After months of study, he performed in a local play that caught his father’s attention. Mark Ruffalo’s approval—“You might be ready now”—gave Keen the confidence to audition for professional roles. Within months, Prime Video cast him as Connor, the twin of lead character Annie in “Sterling Point,” and he secured a part in the comedy “Nimrods.”

## Why it matters
Keen’s trajectory illustrates how the pandemic created unexpected entry points into entertainment, especially for those with industry connections but no prior experience. Streaming platforms like Prime Video are eager to showcase fresh faces, and a recognizable surname can open doors, yet the audience’s response will hinge on genuine talent. His story also underscores the growing blend of fashion, performance, and digital media as young creators navigate multiple disciplines.

## How to think about it
If you’re considering a career pivot, treat downtime as an opportunity to explore adjacent skills—take classes, seek mentorship, and create low‑stakes projects to test your interest. Validate your new path with tangible feedback (e.g., a performance for a trusted mentor) before fully committing. Finally, leverage any existing networks responsibly, but let your work speak for itself.

<Callout type="warning">A famous surname is not a substitute for sustained effort and craft development.</Callout>
