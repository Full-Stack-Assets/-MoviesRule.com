Netflix’s latest licensing win reshapes the streaming landscape: the platform has locked in a five‑year, $500 million co‑exclusive agreement with AMC Global Media for The Walking Dead universe. The deal brings the original series, its six spinoffs and a total of 371 episodes to Netflix’s library across the U.S., UK, Italy, Australia, New Zealand and other territories. By securing both the flagship show and its extensions, Netflix aims to deepen its horror‑drama catalog and attract long‑time fans. The partnership also grants AMC+ a concurrent streaming window, making the arrangement truly co‑exclusive. Industry observers see this as a litmus test for how legacy franchises can fuel subscriber growth.

<Callout type="takeaway">Netflix’s $500 million deal guarantees The Walking Dead universe will anchor its library for the next five years.</Callout>

## What happened
Netflix will pay $500 million for a five‑year co‑exclusive license to the entire Walking Dead franchise, including the original series and six spinoffs. The agreement was announced alongside AMC Global Media’s second‑quarter earnings, which fell short of Wall Street expectations but prompted the company to raise its full‑year guidance, citing the streaming deal as a catalyst.

The library consists of 371 episodes of the original show plus Fear the Walking Dead, The Walking Dead: Daryl Dixon, Dead City, The Ones Who Live, World Beyond and Tales of The Walking Dead. These titles will be available on Netflix in the United States, the United Kingdom, Italy, Australia, New Zealand and additional markets, while AMC+ will retain a simultaneous streaming window.

For Netflix, the move comes amid heightened investor scrutiny of engagement metrics, as many of its original titles have seen ratings declines in later seasons. Securing a proven, long‑running franchise is intended to bolster subscriber retention and attract new viewers.

## Why it matters
The deal underscores Netflix’s strategy of bolstering its catalog with established IP rather than relying solely on new original productions. By locking in a franchise that has generated a dedicated fan base for over a decade, Netflix can leverage the “Netflix effect” to revive viewership on legacy content. The co‑exclusive nature also signals a willingness to share valuable assets with a competitor (AMC+), potentially reshaping how streaming rights are negotiated in the future. For AMC Global Media, the infusion of $500 million provides a financial cushion that can fund future expansions of the zombie universe.

## How to think about it
Treat the Walking Dead acquisition as a strategic anchor rather than a standalone growth engine. Evaluate the deal’s impact on subscriber acquisition, retention, and churn by measuring viewership spikes during key release windows (e.g., new spinoff premieres). Balance the cost against the franchise’s historical performance and its ability to drive cross‑selling of related merchandise and spin‑off titles. Finally, monitor how the co‑exclusive arrangement influences negotiations with other content owners who may seek similar shared‑window deals.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the deal will translate into lower subscription fees; Netflix may maintain current pricing while absorbing the $500 million cost.</Callout>
