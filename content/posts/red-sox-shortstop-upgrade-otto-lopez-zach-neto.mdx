The Boston Red Sox are actively scouting a shortstop upgrade as the August 3 trade deadline looms. With Andruw Monasterio holding the position and Trevor Story and Marcelo Mayer still rehabbing, the infield is a clear need. Reports indicate the club has narrowed its focus to Miami’s Otto Lopez and versatile infielder Zach Neto. An upgrade could solidify the lineup and keep the team in the playoff hunt.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Red Sox’s pursuit of Otto Lopez and Zach Neto could be the decisive upgrade needed for a playoff run.</Callout>

## What happened
The Red Sox front office, led by Craig Breslow, has expressed concrete interest in Otto Lopez, who currently leads MLB with 138 hits and 27 doubles while batting .329. Lopez’s 2026 season features nine homers, a .853 OPS, and 22 stolen bases, marking career highs across the board.

In addition to Lopez, the team is evaluating Zach Neto, a versatile infielder known for solid defensive instincts and the ability to contribute in multiple roles. While Neto’s offensive numbers are modest, his flexibility offers the Red Sox depth as they await the health returns of Story and Mayer.

Other notable shortstops—Jeremy Peña, CJ Abrams, and Elly De La Cruz—are off the table, leaving Lopez and Neto as the primary realistic options.

## Why it matters
Upgrading the shortstop spot addresses both defensive stability and offensive production, two areas that have hampered Boston’s consistency. A player like Lopez adds a high‑contact bat and speed, potentially boosting run creation in the final stretch of the season. Securing a reliable defender also eases the pressure on returning injured starters and gives the rotation more run support.

However, the pursuit carries risk: the Red Sox may need to part with top prospects or take on additional salary, and Lopez’s breakout could be difficult to sustain. The decision will shape the team’s competitive window and financial flexibility heading into next season.
