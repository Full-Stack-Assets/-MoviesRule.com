California’s annual TV tax credit program just handed Paramount two of its newest series a combined $38 million in incentives. The Alice Silverstone‑starring ‘Clueless’ received just over $16.3 million, while Viola Davis’s corporate‑drama ‘Ascent’ was granted about $21 million. The awards are part of a $145 million distribution to nine projects announced by the California Film Commission on Monday. The money is intended to keep production in Los Angeles, a market that has seen a sharp decline in jobs and sound‑stage usage.

<Callout type="takeaway">Paramount’s ‘Clueless’ and ‘Ascent’ illustrate how state tax credits are being used to anchor high‑profile series in Los Angeles.</Callout>

## What happened
The California Film Commission confirmed that the two Paramount series were allocated a total of $37.3 million in tax credits—$16.3 million for ‘Clueless’ and $21 million for ‘Ascent’. Both projects are projected to employ over 300 crew members each and to generate $69.4 million and $47 million respectively in qualified expenditures, according to the commission’s briefing.

The awards are part of a larger $750 million annual credits program that this week distributed $145 million to nine series, including Sony’s ‘S.W.A.T. Exiles’, a revival of ‘The Rockford Files’, and several untitled streaming projects. Collectively, the nine productions are expected to spend $608 million in California and log more than 1,000 film days.

## Why it matters
The infusion of tax credits directly addresses the recent loss of over 40,000 production‑related jobs in the state and the under‑utilization of sound stages. By anchoring high‑profile titles, the program aims to keep talent, crews, and ancillary businesses local, which can have a multiplier effect on the regional economy. Politically, the move also softens criticism of Paramount’s pending $111 billion Warner Bros. Discovery merger by showing tangible benefits to the state’s economy.

## How to think about it
For viewers, the tax‑credit wins signal that ‘Clueless’ and ‘Ascent’ will likely have the production values of major network dramas, making them worth watching when they debut in 2027. For industry watchers, the allocations highlight which studios are successfully leveraging state incentives, suggesting where future job opportunities may arise. Streaming platforms should monitor the credit program’s criteria, as securing similar funding can lower acquisition costs and improve ROI on original series.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume future series will automatically receive the same level of support; the program is subject to annual budget adjustments.</Callout>
