Peter Jackson's second‑feature horror, The Frighteners, has surged to No. 6 on Shudder’s Top 10, surprising both longtime fans and casual viewers. The 1996 film, originally rushed into a summer release, struggled at the box office despite generally favorable reviews. Over three decades it has cultivated a devoted cult following that kept the title alive in genre conversations. Its recent climb aligns with the movie’s 30th anniversary and a growing appetite for retro horror on niche streaming services. This resurgence underscores how streaming platforms can rewrite the commercial legacy of overlooked genre films.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Frighteners proves that streaming can revive cult horror films and give them new commercial life.</Callout>

## What happened
Co‑written and directed by Jackson, The Frighteners introduced Michael J. Fox as a reluctant ghost‑hunter in a story that blended comedy, thriller and supernatural elements. Though critics were generally favorable, the film’s rushed summer debut in 1996 hampered its box‑office performance, leaving it as an underrated entry in Jackson’s catalog.

On the occasion of its 30th anniversary, the movie entered Shudder’s Top 10, landing at No. 6. The climb has been driven by algorithmic promotion, word‑of‑mouth among horror fans, and renewed interest in Jackson’s early work, pushing the title above several newer releases.

## Why it matters
The chart surge illustrates how niche streaming services can give older, under‑appreciated titles a second commercial life, altering how success is measured beyond theatrical receipts. For Shudder, featuring a classic from a globally recognized director bolsters its brand credibility and attracts subscribers seeking both legacy and contemporary horror. For Jackson’s filmography, the renewed visibility re‑positions The Frighteners as a pivotal experiment that foreshadowed his later genre‑blending projects.

## How to think about it
Viewers can treat the resurgence as an invitation to explore the evolution of horror filmmaking, noting how The Frighteners blends slapstick comedy with supernatural suspense—a template Jackson refined in later projects. Curators should balance classic titles with fresh voices to avoid a nostalgia echo chamber, using data‑driven insights to schedule older films during anniversary windows when audience interest peaks.

<Callout type="warning">Chart positions on niche platforms are fluid; a high ranking today does not guarantee sustained popularity.</Callout>
