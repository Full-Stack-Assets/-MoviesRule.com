Monica Bellucci stole the spotlight at the 79th Locarno Film Festival, where the Luca Guadagnino‑produced drama Ketticè premiered. The Italian coming‑of‑age film, written and directed by Giovanni Tortorici, is set in Palermo in 2012, a period marked by Silvio Berlusconi’s media‑political dominance. It follows 16‑year‑old Giulio, a listless teen whose friendship with the confident Ketty offers a brief escape from his stagnant life. Bellucci’s portrayal of Giulio’s mother—a woman trapped by societal expectations and prone to yelling—adds a raw, generational tension that resonates beyond the screen. The performance signals a bold shift for the actress and hints at the film’s potential appeal on streaming platforms.

<Callout type="takeaway">Bellucci’s volatile mother embodies the frustration of a social role, anchoring Ketticè’s critique of generational disconnect.</Callout>

## What happened
Ketticè debuted at Locarno’s 79th edition, featuring a cast led by Salvatore Gallina, Rachele Testagrossa, and Monica Bellucci. The film’s cinematography is by Camilla Cattabriga, with editing by Marco Costa, and international sales are handled by PiperPlay. Set in 2012 Palermo, the story tracks Giulio’s aimless days until he meets Ketty, whose confidence briefly lifts his gloom. Their bond becomes a refuge from the judgmental adult world.

During the press conference, Bellucci explained that her character “is the victim of a social role,” using snacks and a constant television presence to mask her frustration. She described the mother as someone who “forgot about her femininity,” often yelling, which creates a tense, almost humiliating dynamic with her son. The film’s title refers to Ketty’s nickname in Sicilian dialect, underscoring the regional texture of the narrative.

## Why it matters
Bellucci’s turn away from glamorous roles toward a raw, domestic figure highlights a broader trend of veteran actors embracing complex, socially grounded characters. The film’s focus on generational alienation mirrors contemporary debates about youth disenfranchisement in Italy and beyond, making it culturally resonant. For streaming services, Ketticè offers a prestige‑driven title that can attract cinephile subscribers seeking international, festival‑circuit content.
